How Much Does Water Damage Cost in West Des Moines?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in West Des Moines?

Typical Cost at a Glance

Typical West Des Moines water damage projects cost from $1,500 up to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in West Des Moines, IA move the numbers up or down. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Failed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterSewage, river flooding, storm surgeAbout $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Drives the Price

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. Here is what a contractor looks at when pricing a job.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Handling is least expensive when the water is clean. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. A leak found in an hour costs far less than one found after a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Cost by Common Job

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in West Des Moines. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Costs are highest for basement water removal when the space is finished.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in West Des Moines?

The answer turns on your policy and on what caused the damage. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

Coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Months of dripping from a fitting may be labeled a maintenance issue by the insurer. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. The deductible is a factor as well. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

How to Keep the Bill Down

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. Learn the location of the main valve ahead of time, and turn it off the moment a leak shows up.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. It can take as little as 24 to 48 hours for mold to start. Once it does, the job gets longer and more expensive.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

If the power has not been cut, never wade into standing water, and avoid any water that could carry sewage. The risk of being shocked or getting sick outweighs anything you might save. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

How to Get an Exact Price

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.
