No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at West Des Moines Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Our Structural Drying Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose West Des Moines Restoration:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.
