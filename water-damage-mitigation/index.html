The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. In West Des Moines, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. In fact there are two separate stages. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In cold months, a heated house protects pipes from freezing, even while you travel.
  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Find the main water valve and make sure it has not seized.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Each decision rests on meter readings and our training, never on whether something seems dry to the eye or hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. The result is a file documenting both the damage and the steps taken. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • The pipe was repaired by a plumber, and the water it released is still there.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.
