Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in West Des Moines

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In West Des Moines, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Identify the Category

Before any cleaning, our crew confirms whether this is supply water or water from the drain and bowl, and sets up containment and protective measures to match.

2

Extract and Remove

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.
