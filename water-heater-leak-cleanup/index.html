Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in West Des Moines

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In West Des Moines, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Garage Wall Base

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
