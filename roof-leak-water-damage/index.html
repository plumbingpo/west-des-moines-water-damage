Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in West Des Moines

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Inside the Walls

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Mold in the Attic

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Drywall Ceilings

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Insulation in the Attic

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.
