Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in West Des Moines

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Water depth, along with the square footage that flooded.
  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • Whether a temporary pump is needed to keep groundwater down during the work.
  • How contaminated the water is, because the category drives disposal and disinfection.
