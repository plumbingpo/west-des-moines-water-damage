Crawl Space Water Removal in West Des Moines

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Some wet soil following a big storm happens often, yet water that pools is a problem and needs attention. Mold and wood rot both follow when as little as one or two inches sit there raising the humidity. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
Within 24 to 48 hours, growth can start on wet joists and the paper facing of batts. A crawl space is dark and still, which gives it little chance to dry on its own. Removing the water and lowering the humidity quickly is the best way to limit growth. If we find mold on the framing, it gets cleaned and an antimicrobial is applied.
The pump out itself is normally done in a few hours. Expect 3 to 5 days after that for the lumber, with dehumidification and air movement going the whole time. Heavy timbers, bare soil without a vapor barrier, or weeks of standing water may stretch that out. Meter readings decide when we are done, rather than a set number of days.
Going in while water is pooled is a risk we advise against. Many homes have electrical lines in that space, so the water itself may be live. There can be sewage or bacteria from the ground in it too, plus tight quarters and stale air. A look through the access door with a flashlight is enough before you call for an inspection.
A shop vac can handle a small puddle of clean water. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. Crawl spaces are also tight, dark and may have live wiring or sewage in the water. Professional pumping and drying is the safer choice once the water is more than a thin puddle.
