Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in West Des Moines

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Drips coming out of a ceiling light, air vent or smoke alarm
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Soft, bowed or sagging spots you can feel with a gentle push
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
